Off-Grid Power

Three energy sources. All under 5W. All capable of mining Quartz.

☀️

Solar Mining

A 5V 1W solar panel (100×60mm, ~$3) powers a single ESP32 miner in direct sunlight. Pair with a supercapacitor for cloud buffering. Scale to a backyard array of 10 panels and you've got a 10-node cluster running on free photons.

1W = 1 miner · $3 entry

🌬️

Wind Harvesting

Small vertical-axis wind turbines (5-15W) charge a LiFePO4 battery that feeds ESP32 nodes. A single turbine can power 4-12 miners in steady wind. Ideal for coastal or ridge-line deployments where solar is inconsistent.

5-15W = 4-12 miners

🔋

LiFePO₄ Storage

LiFePO₄ (LFP) cells are the recommended storage chemistry — thermally stable, 2,000+ cycle life, and safe for unattended outdoor deployments. A single 3.2V 10Ah cell stores 32Wh: enough to mine through 26 hours of darkness on one node.

32Wh = 26h night mining

Piezoelectric Harvesting

Vibration energy from road traffic, footsteps, or machinery — another way to power Quartz miners without a wall outlet.

🟢

How It Works

Piezoelectric discs (PZT-5H, Ø35mm) generate AC voltage when mechanically stressed. Road vibrations, footstep impacts, or machinery oscillations produce 1-20V AC per disc. Six discs in parallel yield ~0.5-3mW of harvestable power — enough to mine Quartz in short bursts with a supercapacitor buffer.

⚙️

Power Chain

6× PZT-5H discs → Schottky bridge rectifier → smoothing capacitor → LTC3588-1 buck-boost → supercapacitor bank (2× 10F, 72J) → HT7333 LDO → ESP32 at 3.3V. Optional 5V solar panel supplements the supercapacitor input.

📊

Power Budget

ESP32 active mining: ~180mA @ 3.3V = 594mW

Deep sleep: ~10µA · Light sleep: ~0.8mA

At 3mW harvest rate, the duty cycle is ~1% active (30 seconds mining, 50 minutes charging). Enough to produce a block every few hours on vibration alone.

Intermittent Mining Protocol

Quartz firmware is designed for intermittent power. The miner doesn't assume constant electricity — it harvests, mines, and sleeps in cycles.

🔍

1. Energy Detection

The ESP32 monitors supercapacitor voltage via its ADC. While in deep sleep (~10µA), the RTC periodically wakes the chip to sample the buffer level. If voltage is below the boot threshold, the chip returns to sleep immediately — no wasted cycles.

⚡

2. Boot Threshold

When the supercapacitor reaches 4.5V, the full system boots: WiFi/BLE radio initializes, the PUF cold-boot sequence reads SRAM entropy, and CrystalHash mining begins. This threshold ensures enough energy for a complete mining + broadcast cycle.

⛏️

3. Mine Until Depleted

The miner runs CrystalHash at full speed (~50 H/s, 594mW). It continues mining until the supercapacitor drops to 3.3V — the minimum for stable ESP32 operation. If a valid block is found during this window, it's signed with WOTS+ and broadcast immediately.

😴

4. Deep Sleep Cycle

At 3.3V, the ESP32 flushes state to flash, enters deep sleep, and waits for the buffer to recharge. The RTC alarm checks voltage every 60 seconds. A 5F supercapacitor at 5V stores ~72J — enough for ~2 minutes of active mining before depletion.

Environmental Impact

Quartz mining has a negligible environmental footprint by design.

🌱

Bitcoin mining consumes an estimated 150 terawatt-hours per year — more than many countries. It relies on grid electricity, much of it from fossil fuels, and generates significant e-waste from short-lived ASIC hardware.

Quartz mining consumes 1.2 watts per node, runs entirely on solar, wind, or vibration harvesting, and uses ESP32 chips with a 10+ year lifespan. A 100-node solar farm draws less power than a single incandescent light bulb. No grid tie. No cooling fans. No noise. No fossil fuels.

Quartz mining is carbon-negative capable: when powered by solar panels that would otherwise not be installed, each node offsets more CO₂ than it could ever produce. The hardware lasts a decade. The energy is free. The environmental cost is essentially zero.
