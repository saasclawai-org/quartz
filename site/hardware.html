Hardware Wallet 🔐

Your ESP32 miner IS your hardware wallet. No separate device to buy.

🔑

Keys Generated On-Chip

Ed25519 keys generated using the ESP32's hardware RNG. Private key stored in encrypted flash. Never leaves the device — not for the phone, not for the browser, not for any server.

📺

On-Device Display

Seed phrase shown on the ESP32 screen itself — not your phone. M5Stack Core's 3.2" TFT (320×240) displays QR codes for seed provisioning and signing confirmation.

✍️

Physical Confirmation

To send coins, your phone builds the transaction and sends it over BLE. The ESP32 shows the amount and recipient on screen. You press the physical button to approve. Then it signs.

♻️

Recoverable

Lose or break your ESP32? Enter your 12-word seed phrase in any Quartz wallet (PWA, Android, or new ESP32). Same keys, same coins. BIP39 checksum catches typos.

Storage Architecture 💾

Three tiers. Zero corruption. Survives power loss.

🔒

Tier 0: On-chip Flash

4-16 MB — Firmware, encrypted keys (NVS). Soldered, encrypted, secure.

⚡

Tier 1: FRAM (SPI)

256 KB, $3 — Chain tip, UTXO root, recent headers. Infinite writes, atomic commits, instant non-volatile. The anchor.

📀

Tier 2: USB Flash (OTG)

8-128 GB, $8 — Full block history via ESP32-S3 USB host. Soldered traces, no SD socket failure.

Why not SD cards?

SD cards fail from physical socket issues (vibration, corrosion), power-loss corruption (cheap controllers cache writes in volatile RAM), and controller crashes under sustained writes. The flash itself is fine — the packaging is the problem. FRAM solves the reliability gap with atomic byte-level writes. USB flash solves the capacity gap with soldered connections.
