🥧 Run a Quartz Node

One tarball, one command, about two minutes. Own your corner of the network.

1. Install → 2. Verify sync → 3. Point miners at it → 🌐 You're the network

Why run your own node?

📚 Your chain, verified

A full copy of the chain on your hardware, kept fresh by peer-to-peer sync — kilobyte batches, every block validated (PoW + linkage + UTXO rules). You don't trust anyone's word; your node checked it.

⛏️ Your miners' gateway

Your ESP32s work and submit through your node on your LAN — not some stranger's server. Rewards pay your miner's wallet address directly in the coinbase: the block itself is the receipt.

🛡️ Network resilience

Every node serves the chain to other nodes and serves miners even if the rest of the network disappears. More nodes = no single point of failure. That's the whole point.

1What you need
  • Any Raspberry Pi (a Pi Zero 2 W is plenty) — or any Linux box, ARM or x86
  • ~80 MB free RAM, <1% CPU. No dependencies — pure Python 3 stdlib, nothing to pip-install
  • An SD card and power. That's the whole bill of materials
2Install

On the Pi (Raspberry Pi OS, DietPi, Ubuntu — anything with Python 3):

curl -LO https://quartzchain.net/downloads/quartz-pi-node.tar.gz
tar xzf quartz-pi-node.tar.gz
cd quartz-pi-node
sudo bash install.sh

The installer copies everything to /opt/quartz-node, enables + starts a quartz-node systemd service (auto-restart, survives reboots), and opens port 21100 in ufw if present.

3Verify it's syncing

Two commands:

curl -s localhost:21100/api/v1/info | python3 -m json.tool
journalctl -u quartz-node -f

You want a JSON response with the current height, and log lines like:

🔄 Synced +2 blocks: 40739 → 40741 from https://quartzchain.net

That's incremental p2p sync — kilobytes per batch, not a giant snapshot re-pull. The node keeps itself current from its peer list automatically; no cron, no babysitting.

4Point your ESP32s at it

No firmware rebuild needed. In your miner's captive portal (first boot, or serial command wifi), set the Node field to your Pi's LAN address:

192.168.1.142          ← your Pi's LAN IP (port 80 default)
192.168.1.142:21100    ← explicit host:port form also works

From then on your miner gets work from — and submits blocks through — your node. See the Setup Guide for flashing and the Download page for firmware images.

5Check your miner's receipts

Miner stats live on whichever node builds the blocks, but the receipts are on-chain — any node can show them:

# chain height + hardware miner count (network view)
curl -s https://quartzchain.net/api/v1/info

# your miner's wallet — every reward is a coinbase on-chain
curl -s https://quartzchain.net/api/v1/address/<DEVICE_ADDRESS>

The device address is shown on the miner's serial output / display QR. The web wallet renders the same history with real dates.

How it actually works

Peer-to-peer sync. The node holds a peer list (QUARTZ_PEERS, comma-separated) and pulls incremental batches from whichever peer is furthest ahead — the seed, your friends' Pis, anything running this software. Foreign blocks pass full consensus validation before being applied. Add your node to other people's peer lists: any node can serve any other.
Relay-first block building. While peers are reachable, your miners' submissions are relayed and blocks are built at the strongest tip in the network. If the network is unreachable, your node switches to building blocks locally on its own synced chain — your miners keep mining straight through an outage. When connectivity returns, the mesh reconverges on whichever branch grew furthest.
Rewards are carried by the block itself. Mining payouts are encoded in the coinbase transaction paying your miner's address — not a database entry on someone's server. Every fully-synced node recognizes the balance. Fully seedless operation is one env var away (QUARTZ_LOCAL_MINE=1).

Optional: make your node public

  • Cloudflare Tunnel (recommended — no port-forwarding): cloudflared tunnel pointed at port 21100, and your Pi serves the chain at quartz-pi.yourdomain
  • Port-forward 21100 on your router, or reverse-proxy 443 → 21100 with nginx/caddy
  • LAN-only is fine too — your own miners and wallet use it directly on your network
⚠️ Never erase_flash a miner. The ESP32's NVS holds its wallet private key — erasing flash destroys the key and any QZ it earned. Re-flashing firmware at 0x0 (merged image) preserves it. Bad WiFi? Serial terminal → type wifi → the captive portal reopens. ESP32 is 2.4 GHz only.
